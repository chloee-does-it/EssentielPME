Measuring your advertising: GA4, Meta pixel and conversions for SMBs (no jargon)

Measuring your advertising means knowing which ad brings calls, bookings or quote requests, and which one burns the budget. For an SMB, three tools are enough: GA4 (free, from Google), the Meta pixel with the Conversions API, and tracking on the actions that matter (call, form, booking). In Quebec, Law 25 frames all of it: consent is required before activating these tools. This guide explains every piece, without jargon, in the order to install them in 2026.

Why the absence of measurement burns your budget

Without measurement, two ads look the same. With measurement, one brings bookings at $8 and the other at $45. And on a $1,500 monthly budget, that difference is one-to-five in results, for the same spend. Exactly the same.

It's also the reason behind the famous “I tried ads, they don't work”. In our audits at SMB Essentials, most abandoned accounts had no conversion tracking at all: impossible to know what was working, therefore impossible to optimize. The campaign didn't fail. It was never steered.

And there's more. Google's and Meta's algorithms optimize toward what you measure: no declared conversions, no learning. Your competitor who measures is training their machine; you aren't. The gap widens every week.

GA4 in plain language

Google Analytics 4 (GA4) is the free tool that records what happens on your site: where visitors come from, what they view, what they do. Four notions are enough.

GA4 termIn plain language
EventAn action on your site: page view, click on your phone number, form submitted.
Conversion (key event)The event that matters for your business: quote request, booking, call. YOU declare it.
Source / mediumWhere the visitor comes from: organic Google, Meta ads, email, direct.
AttributionWhich source GA4 credits for a conversion when the customer saw several channels before acting.

The basic setup is done with Google Tag Manager (GTM): a single code on the site, then every tracking piece is configured without touching the code. The minimum trio for an SMB: clicks on your phone number, form submissions, clicks on the booking button. Everything else is comfort.

The Meta pixel and the Conversions API: why your ads “don't work”

The Meta pixel is a piece of code that tells Facebook and Instagram what visitors from your ads actually do. Without it, Meta optimizes blindly. It shows your ads to people who click, not to people who book.

The problem in recent years: cookie blockers and iOS restrictions make the pixel lose part of the conversions. Meta's answer is the Conversions API (CAPI): the data leaves from your server or your tools (form, booking system, CRM) rather than only from the browser, which gives a more complete measurement, therefore a better-trained algorithm, therefore a lower cost per result. A chain. Three links.

Bare minimum: pixel installed via GTM + a conversion event on the action that matters (form, booking, call).

Recommended level: pixel + Conversions API with deduplication (both send, Meta removes duplicates).

Check every month: does Meta Events Manager show your conversions? A pixel that's been silent for three weeks is a campaign flying blind.

Law 25: consent and advertising, what an SMB must do

Law 25 governs the protection of personal information in Quebec. For your marketing, it comes down to three concrete obligations. This summary is not legal advice; for specific cases, consult the Commission d'accès à l'information (CAI) or a legal advisor.

1. Consent before non-essential cookies

Your site must display a consent banner, and GA4 and the Meta pixel must only activate after a “yes”. Default settings must be the most protective ones.

2. Transparency

A clear privacy policy (what data, why, who it's shared with) and a designated person responsible for the protection of personal information. In an SMB, that's often the owner.

3. Forms

Collect only what's necessary, say what you'll do with the email address (e.g., receive the guide + the newsletter), and offer a simple unsubscribe.

The good news: a compliant site is not a site that measures worse. Google's consent mode and Meta's Conversions API are designed precisely to work within this framework: compliance and performance don't oppose each other, they get installed together, once.

The monthly report that matters: our 6 indicators

A good report fits on one page. It answers a single question: is the advertising paying off? Here are the six indicators we track at SMB Essentials, in this order.

IndicatorThe question it answers
1. Conversions (leads, bookings, calls)How many business opportunities this month?
2. Cost per conversionHow much does each opportunity cost? Is it viable against a customer's value?
3. Spend vs budgetDid we spend what was planned, where it was planned?
4. Split by platformWho delivers: Google, Meta, TikTok? Where to reallocate?
5. Best and worst adsWhat to cut, what to amplify next month?
6. 3-month trendIs it improving? (A single month proves nothing.)

What should never open a report: impressions and reach. They're vanity metrics: big, flattering, and incapable of paying an invoice. They're for diagnosis. Not for the verdict.

FAQ

Is GA4 free?
Yes, entirely, for an SMB's needs. Installation via Google Tag Manager takes one to two hours for a solid basic setup.
What is the difference between the Meta pixel and the Conversions API?
The pixel measures from the visitor's browser; the Conversions API sends data from your systems. Together (with deduplication), they give the most complete measurement, and better costs per result.
Does Law 25 apply to my small business?
Yes: the law covers any business that collects personal information in Quebec, with no size threshold. A consent banner, a privacy policy and a designated privacy officer are the minimum.
What should I track first if I'm not measuring anything today?
Three actions: clicks on your phone number, form submissions and online bookings. With that, you already know which campaign is working.
Does consent make you lose data?
Some, yes, that's the principle. But Google's consent mode and the Conversions API partly compensate, and a measurement that's 80% compliant beats one that's 100% illegal.
